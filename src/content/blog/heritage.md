> A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children...

<figcaption>Proverbs 13:22</figcaption>

I despise yard work. I don't mean mowing the grass, string-trimming, raking leaves, shoveling snow, you know - the easy stuff. Those chores are, indeed, very rewarding and, perhaps even a little cathartic. There's a clear start and finish line and the results of completing the task are predictable. Sure, I know I'll have to repeat the same task again in a week, but it will likely go about the same as it went the last time and I'll be glad I did it.

No, the things I truly do not enjoy are things like pulling weeds, trimming shrubs, pruning trees, repairing sprinkler lines, replacing weed barrier, and the like. Things that require a lot more time - a commodity I can't seem to acquire enough of these days. Something about these types of tasks feels futile; no matter the effort I apply, I know I'm going to have to do the same thing again at a later time. There's just no finality to the matter.

The other day I was removing what seemed like a small forest of shrubs and branches from our property, and as I was loading the trailer, a thought came across my mind that I hadn't yet considered regarding the purpose of what I was doing. Beautifying our little piece of land adds value and can help increase the resale value of our home. To me, our home has always, from day one, not just been a place to live but a means of blessing for our children - it is an essential part of the inheritance I want to leave for them. Value that I add to the home might not benefit me all that much since I have no immediate intention of selling, but it will very likely benefit them twenty or thirty years from now.

This thought gave me some motivation as I was dragging branches to the trailer. I've never worked on my property with the idea that my hard work would benefit someone else; my motivation was always just that "it had to be done." As I meditated on this idea while I worked, I began to feel older, more mature - maybe a bit like a grandfather who realizes that his time is short and his vocation (find a different word) is to leave a great inheritance for his children and grandchildren. It was nice.
